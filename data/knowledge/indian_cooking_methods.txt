INDIAN COOKING METHODS
Source note: general culinary knowledge summarised by the project author.

Tadka (tempering): spices such as cumin and mustard seeds are fried in hot ghee or oil and poured over dal or curries at the end for aroma.
Bhunao: frying onion, tomato and masala until the oil separates from the paste. This builds the base of most North Indian curries.
Dum: slow cooking in a sealed pot on low heat so steam cooks the food, as used in biryani.
Pressure cooking: used for dals, rajma and chickpeas to save time. Soaking beans overnight shortens cooking time.
Blanching spinach for about two minutes before grinding keeps the green colour for palak dishes.
Paneer becomes rubbery if cooked too long. Add it near the end and simmer briefly.
Marinating chicken in curd and spices for 15 to 30 minutes makes it softer and more flavourful.
Substitutions: tofu can replace paneer, oil can replace ghee, and jaggery can replace sugar. Flavour and nutrition will change.
